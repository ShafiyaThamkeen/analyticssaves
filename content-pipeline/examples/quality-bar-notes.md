# Quality bar notes (from a prior HLB HAMT content project)

Distilled from an earlier SugarAI CRM content project's revision history.
These are the recurring corrections that came up, used to calibrate the
four agents. Not a template to copy structurally for every requirement,
each requirement gets its own fresh plan, but the standards below hold
across all of them.

## Structure and templates

- When a sample/reference page or template is provided, match its exact
  section order and named blocks (hero, credential strip, a stats/"proven"
  band, FAQs, CTA, tabbed content, etc.), not a generic substitute
  structure.
- On multi-page projects (e.g. one page per industry), keep the section
  skeleton consistent across pages, but do not let two pages reuse the same
  stats or near-duplicate FAQ questions. Each page's proof points and FAQs
  need to be specific to its own subject.

## Tone and framing

- Don't open a hero or intro section with industry pain points or
  challenges. Lead with the capability and the outcome; save problems for
  where the brief specifically calls for a problem-led section.
- Push subheadings toward specific, technical language for the subject
  rather than generic marketing headers.
- Avoid naming competitors or naming sources inline in on-page copy
  (stats get a footnote/source log entry, not a "according to [competitor]"
  credit inside a stats band).

## SEO and measurement

- Always be ready to state, on request: exact word count, primary keyword
  occurrence count, and the source URL for every stat used. Build these
  into the draft file itself (word count target stated, stats list with
  sources) so this is a lookup, not a re-count, when asked.
- Keyword frequency targets should be explicit numbers (e.g. "primary
  keyword 3-4 times"), not just "used naturally," so pass/fail is
  checkable.
- Word count ranges vary by content type and get tightened through
  iteration (seen ranges: ~1,000-1,200 for a blog/case-study-length page,
  1,500-1,700 and up to 2,000 for a longer industry subpage). Treat the
  range in the brief as the target, not a ceiling to always max out.

## Originality and accuracy

- Run an explicit originality/plagiarism check against reference sites and
  the wider web before calling a draft done, not just a read-through.
- Stats must be traceable to a real, credible, reasonably recent source.
  When asked "what's the source for X," the answer needs to be a real URL,
  not a reconstruction after the fact.
- Don't drop or invent facts from a client transcript or source document;
  check the draft against them explicitly.

## Deliverables

- Final handoff is typically: a Word doc and an HTML page (styled to match
  the site) plus a PDF, ready to send to a developer or manager for
  approval before it goes live. Keep formatting faithful across all three
  so nothing is missing from one version that another has.
